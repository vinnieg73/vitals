Help

Everything Activity Monitor shows, on one screen, in plain words. Each number below says what it means and what yours is now.

What the numbers mean

Memory pressure
How hard macOS is working to fit everything in memory. Low is good. High means your Mac is juggling.
Yours: 78%, strained
Swap
Memory overflow parked on the SSD. Much slower than real memory, and it wears the SSD.
Yours: 6.02 of 7.00 GB
Compressed
Memory macOS has squashed to make room. Cheaper than swap, but it costs a little CPU.
Yours: 3.07 GB
Wired
Memory macOS itself needs and can't move or free. You can't change it.
Yours: 2.71 GB
App memory
What your apps are using right now. This is the part you can free by quitting things.
Yours: 10.84 GB
Cached files
Recently used files kept in memory for speed. macOS gives it back the moment an app needs it.
Yours: 1.21 GB
Energy impact
A rough score of how much power an app draws. Higher drains the battery faster. Over 20 is a lot.
Highest: Claude VM, 38.2
Load average
How many jobs are waiting for the CPU, over 1, 5 and 15 minutes. Under 11 (your core count) is fine.
Yours: 5.2 · 4.8 · 4.6
SSD written today
Data written to your drive since midnight. Lots of swap makes this climb, and SSDs have a lifetime limit.
Yours: 97.4 GB

When memory is tight

  1. Quit Claude when you're not using it. Its sandbox holds about 10 GB. Quitting frees about 11.5 GB.
  2. Close Claude Code sessions you're done with. Each one holds 180 to 340 MB.
  3. Close Safari tabs you don't need. Safari is using 1.62 GB.
  4. Restart now and then. It empties swap. Yours has been up 8 days.
Leave kernel_task and WindowServer alone. They are macOS itself.
